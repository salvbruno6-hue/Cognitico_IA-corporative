# ELO Strategic Recovery and Reversal Lens

## Purpose

A resolved problem is not automatically a successful business outcome. After a resolution, ELO must perform a second-order strategic assessment: **how can the organization recover value, protect itself, reverse an ineffective route, or advance toward the objective?**

This is a decision-support lens, not a new orchestrator, executor or Core.

## Canonical sequence

```text
SITUATION
  ↓
RESOLUTION
  ↓
OUTCOME / RESIDUAL STATE
  ↓
OBJECTIVE CHECK
  ↓
RESIDUAL RISK + OPPORTUNITY
  ↓
STRATEGIC PATHS
  ├─ ADVANCE
  ├─ RECOVER
  ├─ REVERSE
  ├─ PROTECT
  ├─ EXPLOIT
  └─ HANDOFF
  ↓
NEXT MOVE + TRIGGER
  ↓
AUTHORITY CHECK
  ↓
EXECUTE ONLY IF ALREADY AUTHORIZED
  ↓
MONITOR → OUTCOME FEEDBACK → REASSESS
```

## Strategic question after every material resolution

ELO should ask, using available evidence:

1. What changed because the situation was resolved?
2. Did the resolution restore the original objective or only contain the problem?
3. What residual risk remains?
4. What capability, resource, relationship or time window can now be protected?
5. Is the previous route still the best route?
6. Should the organization advance, recover, reverse, protect or exploit?
7. What is the safest next move that increases enterprise value or reduces exposure?
8. What observable signal should trigger a new reassessment?

## Reversal is a strategic capability

Reversal is not failure. It is a governed response when evidence shows that the current route is no longer the best-supported path.

A reversal must:

- preserve the evidence and historical decision;
- identify the assumption or condition that changed;
- avoid rewriting history;
- compare viable alternatives;
- state operational and strategic impacts;
- preserve rollback/recovery criteria;
- respect authorization boundaries;
- generate a new decision state rather than mutating the previous decision.

## Enterprise value orientation

The lens should evaluate consequences beyond the immediate incident, including:

- continuity;
- cash and margin exposure;
- customer commitment;
- capacity utilization;
- bottleneck propagation;
- supplier/customer dependency;
- operational resilience;
- compliance and contractual exposure;
- opportunity cost;
- learning value and reusable capability.

The ELO must not invent these values. Missing inputs become GAP/follow-up conditions.

## Architectural boundaries

- No second Cognitive Core.
- No strategic executor.
- No parallel memory.
- No automatic financial or contractual commitment.
- No silent reversal of a prior decision.
- No promotion of a contextual strategy to Core without the existing Evolution Gate.
- Strategic paths remain recommendations until existing authority permits execution.
- Existing DecisionRecord and OutcomeFeedback mechanisms remain the canonical historical/result boundary.

## Decision semantics

| Condition | Preferred path |
|---|---|
| Stable resolution and objective remains supported | ADVANCE |
| Residual risk can prevent objective | RECOVER |
| Current route is no longer best supported | REVERSE |
| Exposure remains material | PROTECT |
| Validated opportunity appears after resolution | EXPLOIT |
| Authority/evidence is insufficient for a material next step | HANDOFF |

A case may contain multiple paths. The recommended path must be explicit and evidence-bound.

## Strategic maturity

The ELO should evolve from:

`solve the problem`

to:

`solve → understand the residual state → protect the enterprise → choose the best route → advance → observe → learn`.

This converts resolution into an opportunity for strategic movement rather than treating incident closure as the endpoint.
